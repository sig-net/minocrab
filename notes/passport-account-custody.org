#+TITLE: passport PR #184 — the account custody contract in MinoCrab: corpus entry, lint results, improvement list
#+DATE: 2026-10-15

Context: midnightntwrk/passport PR #184 (nicolasdp/minocrab-account-custody, head 531580c) is an independent MinoCrab port of Midnight's account custody contract — 52 state-changing entrypoints, a 25-field ledger, JubJub/k256/P256 device and grant authorisation, recovery, shielded and unshielded custody — measured against the unchanged Compact reference on the same ZKIR 3.1.0-rc.1 backend. dmd asked (2026-10-15) for both halves in the corpus and for a list of improvements to the code or to MinoCrab that lower k, or make the result safer or more ergonomic. This note is that list, with the evidence each item rests on.

AUTHORSHIP, recorded at dmd's request: the Compact (=reference/contracts/*.compact=, byte copies of passport =contract/= at 0d7bb0bc) is human-written. The Rust port, its measure harness and the evidence are NOT human-written (machine-written, by the PR author's agent). Read the two halves with that in mind: the Compact carries the design reasoning in its comments; the Rust mirrors the Compact's transcript and inherits its decisions.

* 2. What the port had to work around — the MinoCrab gaps, each with the proposed API

Each item names the corpus copy (=corpus/src/passport-account-custody/experiments/minocrab-account-custody/=, abbreviated =port/=) where the workaround lives. Ordered by how much the port had to write.

1. *=kernel.caller= does not exist.* =port/crates/account-custody/src/caller.rs= hand-assembles thirteen Impact op sequences as raw byte immediates (=0x32 dup context; 0x60 idx caller; type; eq; branch …=) and reads six public-transcript inputs for the nullable =PublicAddress=. It is the ONE place a typo (a wrong context index, a wrong cell-type tag) is silent until the ledger rejects the transaction. Propose =kernel::caller(c) -> Maybe<ContractAddress<Public>>= in =crates/minocrab-std/src/v3/kernel.rs= beside =self_address= (kernel.rs:158 is the pattern), lowering exactly the port's sequence and carrying its property in the docs: the caller is the ledger's answer, never a private witness. The nine grant-withdraw circuits are the consumers.
2. *secp256r1 has types but no surface.* Core has =Secp256r1PointT/BaseT/ScalarT= with =EqAddTy=, =MulTy=, =Bytes32ConvTy=, =PointTy= (v3.rs:74-150) but =GeneratorScalarTy= is implemented only for JubJub and k1 (v3.rs:128-131), and std has no =Secp256r1Point=/=Secp256r1Scalar= argument wrappers, no =Secp256r1EcdsaSignature=, no =secp256r1_ecdsa_verify=. So =port/…/p256.rs= declares the argument types through a macro over =CircuitAbi=, builds the P-256 GENERATOR from its affine hex constants (two =from_bytes32= + =from_coordinates=, an on-curve check for a constant) and writes ECDSA by hand (inv, two muls, two =ec_mul=, =into_coordinates=, =from_bytes32=, =assert_eq=). ZKIR 3.1 supports =EcMulGenerator= for =Secp256r1Scalar= both off- and in-circuit (ir_vm.rs:646 and :1326) — BUT in-circuit it is =assign_fixed(generator)= followed by the same =ec_mul_incircuit= as a variable point, not a fixed-base routine, so switching saves the constant's construction (hundreds of rows), not a k. Propose: =impl GeneratorScalarTy for Secp256r1ScalarT=; std wrappers mirroring the k1 ones; =secp256r1_ecdsa_verify= mirroring =secp256k1_ecdsa_verify= (std v3.rs:2265); and the simulator's P256 generator arm, which the port's forked simulator also lacks (=port/bin/measure/src/simulator.rs:840=).
3. *No hash-preimage builder.* =port/…/auth.rs= =Preimage= is 50 lines that build a FAB =Alignment= and a slot list by hand over =hash::persistent_hash_compact=, with =.bytes()/.uint(wire, byte_width)/.field()/.blob()/.append()/.tag()= — because std's =persistent_hash<T: CircuitBorsh>= needs a type and the =_compact= form needs the raw alignment. Every one of the ~20 hashes in the port goes through it. It is the ergonomic win we lack; propose lifting it into std as =hash::Preimage= with one change: =uint= takes a =Uint<BITS>= rather than a bare field wire plus a byte count, so the limb's proven bound and its declared width are the same object. That is the taint lint's §B3 class — a hash limb wider than its slot — made unrepresentable instead of audited after the fact.
4. *The =Check= vocabulary was bypassed.* The port writes booleans as field arithmetic: =c.not(x.field())=, =c.mul(a, b)= for AND, =cond_select(a, 1, b)= for OR (=either=, grants.rs), =cond_select(a, b, 1)= for IMPLIES (=implies=), two =test_eq= and a =mul= for =B32= equality (=bytes_eq=, auth.rs). Two causes, both ours: =Check= has no =implies=, and =Check::or= lowers by De Morgan as three =not= and a =mul= (predicate.rs:629) where one =select= does the job. Propose =Check::implies=, lower =Or= as =select(a, 1, b)= (document the cost as the file does for =or= today), and =B32::eq(c, other) -> Bool= — the port needed it eleven times. None of this moves k; it removes a reason to leave the typed surface.
5. *Ledger read-modify-write on =Uint= cells.* =port/…/lib.rs= =increment= is read → =add= → =Uint::from_field_checked= (a 64-bit range check) → write, called three to four times per circuit (=round=, =auth_nonce=, =inbox_count=, =device_count=). =Counter= has =increment= (ledger.rs:1940); =LedgerCell<Uint<N>>= has nothing. Propose =LedgerCell<Uint<N, Public>>::add(c, k)= with the checked semantics spelled out. Separately, =deposit_unshielded= writes the exists/not-exists arms as two independent =when=s where =when(..).otherwise(..)= already exists (core v3.rs:1800) — one negation, no second guard — and rows went 331 → 340 against Compact; a =LedgerMap::upsert_add= would make the idiom one call.
6. *Witness declaration.* =port/…/custody.rs= =held_coin= declares six raw witnesses and range-checks them by hand because there is no =c.witness::<T: CircuitArg>()=. Propose =Circuit3::private_arg::<T>()= reusing =CircuitArg::declare= with a witness source. Likewise the =Scalar= newtype (custody.rs) exists only because std has no =Field= argument type.
7. *=.stale(c)= on every read.* The port has well over a hundred =read(c).stale(c)= / =lookup(c, ..).stale(c)= sites and NOT ONE site that uses the =Assumed= machinery for its purpose. M42's design holds (the staleness is real), but this is the first external contract and it voted with its feet; either a contract-level =read_stale= spelling or a review of what the per-site acknowledgement buys is due.
8. *One disclosure label for everything.* Every disclosed value is =disclose_as::<Mutation>= ("reference operation disclosures"). That is correct for parity with a Compact contract that has no labels, but it means the per-recipient disclosure model (PR #16) carried zero information here. Worth a worked example of what labels WOULD say for this contract (device entry vs grant id vs coin fields) — the model's first real test.
9. *An executor bug, fixed in the fork — upstream it.* =port/bin/measure/src/executor.rs= is our =crates/minocrab-sim/src/v3/exec.rs= with one change (lines 712-720 of theirs): decoded FAB atoms are =normalize()=d, so an absent union arm (a zero =bytes<32>= standing for unit) decodes as the ledger expects, plus two regression tests (=unused_union_arm_decodes_as_ledger_unit=, =normalisation_preserves_nonzero_value_and_alignment=). That is a correctness fix to our executor; take it with the tests.
10. *The simulator fork confirms the migration's hot path.* =port/bin/measure/src/simulator.rs= is our =minocrab-sim/src/v3.rs= re-based on the 3.1 backend's typed AST to get =to_bytes/from_bytes/bytes_into_natives/bytes_from_natives= and P256 — exactly the four instructions the zkir-3.1 branch's finding 2 said compactc now emits everywhere. The refusal arm on this branch is on the hot path for all 52 of these artifacts.

* 3. k and rows: where the levers are

First the ground rule, from the PR's own evidence (=port/evidence/report.md=): k is NOT a function of ordinary rows alone. =remove_device_with_p256= is k=18 at 125,404 rows while =withdraw_unshielded_with_p256= is k=17 at 126,284; =publish_recovery_session_with_p256= is k=18 at 127,922. The backend sizes k from its tables and reserved rows as well. So a row saving is a k saving only when measured — the PR's =bin/measure= harness (=costs= subcommand) is the instrument, and follow-up 1 makes it runnable here.

Headroom, Rust side, rows below the next power of two (indicative only, per the rule above): =withdraw_shielded_with_k256= 67,701 and =withdraw_shielded_to_contract_with_k256= 67,766 sit 2.2k over 2^16 with reserved rows pushing the real threshold to roughly 61-62k (inferred from the P256 pairs above), so a 6-7k cut (9%) is what 17 → 16 costs there; =issue_grant_with_k256= 79,423 and the three grant withdrawals at 86k need 20k+; every P256 circuit at k=17 is 58-65k over 2^16 and no lever below the backend reaches that.

The levers, with the op-count evidence from the compactc artifacts (=corpus/zkir/passport-account-custody/…/account/zkir/=):

1. *Coordinate-byte reuse, on the Compact side.* =add_device_with_k256= as compactc emits it has 9 =into_coordinates= and 10 =to_bytes=; the port computes them twice (=Auth::k256=, auth.rs) and reuses the bytes for the weak-key guard, the entry commitment, the boot commitment and the challenge. That is the 61,809 → 52,002 difference (9.8k rows, 17 → 16) on ten k256 circuits, and most of the 129,887 → 124,132 on P256. Compact can do the same: compute =secp256k1PointX(pk) as Bytes<32>= once per circuit and pass the bytes to =derive_device_entry_with_k256= / =derive_boot_commitment_with_k256= / the challenge builder instead of the point (account.compact:451, :501, :725, :744 each re-derive). The PR says so; the op counts put a number on it.
2. *Grant envelope specialisation* (done in the port, =Auth::verify_grant=): spend grants require envelope 0, so the connector digest and its second =persistent_hash= are unreachable there. Portable to Compact's grant arm the same way.
3. *The JubJub small-order check is two scalar multiplications for a cofactor test.* =ecMul(pk, 8) != ecMulGenerator(0)= (account.compact:407, :861, :1987, :2560-2561; the port's =live_key= and =recovery::live= mirror it) — a ZKIR =ec_mul= is a full-width scalar loop whatever the scalar's value, and =ec_mul_generator(0)= is a second full loop to produce the constant (0, 1). Replace with three =add(p, p)= doublings (JubJub is twisted Edwards; the unified addition formula handles doubling, and =EqAddTy for JubjubPointT= already exposes it) compared against an =assign_constant= identity: two scalar muls → three point adds and a constant, on every JubJub-authorised circuit and three times in =recover_submit=. The Compact comment at :2545 explains why the check exists (defence in depth, and routing a stored point through a curve op so the circuit constrains it) — three doublings satisfy both purposes. Propose std =JubjubPoint::assert_not_small_order(c)= with the lowering recorded, and the Compact equivalent as a suggestion back to passport. Measure before claiming the k: =publish_recovery_session_with_jubjub= (36,917, k=16) and =recover_submit= (31,295, k=15) are the ones where it could matter.
4. *The identity write =ecMul(pending_recovery_pk, 0)=* (account.compact:2864, :2920; the port's =clear_pending=) is another full scalar mul to produce a constant. The Compact comment at :2851 gives the reason — a bare point literal leaves the JubJub gadget disabled and key generation fails on the k256 cancel twin, which does no other JubJub op. In MinoCrab that is a backend fact we can encode once: a typed constant via =assign_constant= plus a documented "gadget-enabling" op where a circuit would otherwise have none, instead of a scalar mul at every site. Small rows, but it is also a correctness trap worth a std-level answer.
5. *Base64 in-circuit.* Both sides decompose 32 challenge bytes to 256 bits — Compact's =byte_bits= is 8 comparisons and 7 selects per byte (438 =less_than=, 990 =cond_select=, 292 =div_mod_power_of_two= in every P256 artifact), the port's =base64= 256 one-bit =div_mod_power_of_two= and the same 43-way sextet mapping. The sextets of a 3-byte group are three =div_mod_power_of_two(group, 6)= on the 24-bit value: 33 ops in place of 256 bit splits and their recombination. Order 1-2k rows per P256 circuit; it only buys a k where a circuit is already at a boundary, and the two k=18 P256 circuits under 2^17 are there for a non-row reason (see the ground rule), so measure first. A std =base64url::<N>= would make the cheaper form the default.
6. *P256 ECDSA itself* is two variable-base P-256 multiplications in foreign-field arithmetic and nothing expressible above ZKIR changes that; a double-scalar (Shamir) or MSM instruction would be a backend request, and we never compile below ZKIR.
7. *Where the port is WORSE than Compact* (rows, same k): =recover_submit= +859, =publish_recovery_session_with_jubjub= +426, =recover_cancel_with_jubjub= +361, =recover_finalise= +83, =deposit_unshielded= +9, =withdraw_unshielded_with_grant_jubjub= +51. The deposit one is item 2.5 (two =when=s). The recovery ones want the op-histogram diff of follow-up 1 before guessing; candidates are =increment='s checked add where Compact folds the cast, and =live= called three times in =recover_submit= (item 3.3 recovers that and more).

* 4. Safety: the lint over the oracle, and what reading the port showed

The taint lint (=cargo run -p minocrab-taint --example taint_corpus -- corpus/zkir/passport-account-custody=) over the 52 compactc artifacts: 35 fired, 213 findings, in four classes.

- *180: our =to_bytes= rule, a false positive of the parked migration.* Every k256 and P256 artifact, six or seven each, never a JubJub one: a =bytes_into_natives= whose input came from =to_bytes= of an =into_coordinates= output gets both limbs bounded at 248 bits, so the 1-byte limb that feeds a =bytes<32>= hash slot reads "only proven ≤ 248 — required ≤ 8". taint.rs:398-406 says why: =ToBytes= is deliberately not marked as a 32-byte producer because a secp POINT gives 33 bytes and "the input's type is not visible here". It is visible one step back — =into_coordinates= outputs are base-field elements, =from_bytes= to a base/scalar type likewise — so a small type set (field-like wires) marks exactly those =to_bytes= outputs as =bytes32= and the 180 go. This is the "taint rules promoted from precautionary to load-bearing" line of the 3.1 bump's finding 2, now with a number.
- *18: the kernel caller read under a variable guard.* The nine grant-withdraw circuits, two limbs each: the =%caller.*= public inputs read inside the =restricted= branch (account.compact's caller-bound grant check; the port's =check_scope=). Same class as the vault's guarded =kernel.self()= reads (taint-lint.org §8 class A), same warrant question: a false guard pushes zeros and the hash sees a free wire. compactc's own residue, not the port's.
- *15: the base64 output's 12-byte limb in the =bytes<43>= client-data hash.* The 43 sextet characters are each a chain of =cond_select= over constants 45..122; the lint cannot bound a select whose operands are bounded, so the packed limb is "not provably bounded". Either the rule grows "select of two bounded operands is bounded by their max" — true, and it discharges this class — or it stands as compactc's residue. Our own base64 (item 3.5) would hit the same rule.
- *The port's own IR is not linted*: it was not built here (§1). Follow-up 1.

From reading the port (not from any tool): =from_field_unchecked= appears about fifteen times and each source is bounded on inspection (a =test_eq= output, an =assert_bits= result, a constant, a ledger read); =Preimage::uint= is where the author's byte width and the wire's bound can disagree silently (item 2.3 fixes the API); =caller.rs= is the only unchecked opcode sequence (item 2.1); the P256 argument wrappers borrow the k1 ABI atoms (=p256.rs:30=) which is right only while the two ABIs coincide; and the port carries the Compact's documented Secp256k1Point-identity subtlety (account.compact:487-525) faithfully as a byte compare on both coordinates.

* 7. As measured (2026-10-15): the port built against our crates, the levers costed, the helpers upstreamed

The port builds unchanged against main's crates (its MinoCrab pin IS main), and =minocrab_sim::v3::cost= reproduces the PR's k and rows for all 52 circuits to the row — so we have the PR's instrument locally. A micro-benchmark of single ops under that model is =notes/op-costs.org= on the =passport-upstream= branch; the three numbers that reorder §3 are: =div_mod_power_of_two= costs 148 rows at ANY shift (a byte extraction is fifty byte range checks), =jubjub_scalar_from_native= costs 402 even for a constant, and a 64-byte =persistent_hash= costs 3,750 — which compactc spends on a constant domain tag in every challenge-bearing circuit.

What that made of §3: the P-256 circuits spend ~50k of their 124k rows on 342 byte extractions (base64 by one-bit splits, a 37-byte authenticator split into bytes to read three of them, the origin check); the JubJub cofactor check is ~2.2k rows for what three doublings do in ~30; the constant tag hash is 3.75k per circuit on both sides. The P-256 ECDSA (two 22k-row multiplications) and the SHA blocks of the protocol's preimages are the floor nothing above ZKIR moves. Item 3.3 of the list above understated the cofactor saving (the scalar conversion is most of it) and item 3.5 understated base64 by an order of magnitude.

Upstreamed on the =passport-upstream= branch (worktree =~/minocrab-passport=, off main, unpushed), each with a simulation test: =hash::Preimage= with a typed =uint= and an off-circuit fold of all-constant preimages; =base64url_32= (62 splits, not 286); =JubjubPoint::times_cofactor= / =is_identity= / =assert_not_small_order=; =kernel::caller= byte-for-byte compactc's lowering; =B32::eq=; =Check::implies=; =LedgerCell<Uint<N>>::add=; the executor's FAB-atom normalisation with the port's two regression tests. Not upstreamed: the secp256r1 generator trait (main's backend has no P-256 generator arm; zkir-3.1's does).

The port with those substituted, same cost model: total rows 3,656,014 → 2,948,966 (−19.3%); k lowered on 14 of 52 circuits — every P-256 circuit at k=18 (nine) to 17, and six JubJub circuits one step down (=append_inbox=, =remove_device=, =publish_recovery_session= 16→15; the two shielded grant withdrawals 17→16; =recover_finalise= 12→11). The k256 =withdraw_shielded= pair stays at 17 (67.7k → 64.0k; the threshold is ~61-62k and the rest is ECDSA and SHA). The port-side substitutions are =notes/passport-port-improvements.patch= here (a diff over the corpus copy), for sending back to passport; the Compact-side equivalents (§3.1-3.3, plus "split bytes by six, not by one" for the WebAuthn module) are now quantified.

Answer to "are they already doing about as well as you can": no — about a fifth of the rows and a k step on 14 circuits were available above ZKIR, and the three sources are generic (byte extraction, constant hashing, the cofactor idiom), not passport-specific. The floor after that is the backend's: P-256 at ~44k for the ECDSA alone, k256 at 23.4k, SHA at 1.9k a block.
