#+TITLE: ZKIR op costs under Midnight's cost model, and what they bought on the passport port
#+DATE: 2026-10-15

Measured 2026-10-15 with =minocrab_sim::v3::cost= (Midnight's own =IrSource::model()=, the k and row count the prover will see) on one-op circuits, baseline (empty circuit) subtracted. Backend: midnight-ledger 04c9c5d's zkir-v3, the one main is pinned to; the passport PR #184 numbers it reproduces were produced on midnight-zkir 3.1.0-rc.1, and every one of the 52 circuits came out identical to the row, so the two models agree on these ops. The table is the thing to have open when a circuit is 2k rows over a power of two.

* 2. What the table said about the passport port, and what was lifted into std

The port (passport PR #184, machine-written, 52 circuits) built against this branch's crates reproduces the PR's k and rows exactly. Its op histograms against the table:

- *P-256 circuits spent ~50k of 124k rows on byte extraction*: 342 =div_mod_power_of_two= per circuit — 256 of them one-bit splits for base64 (the Compact reference's =byte_bits= does the same with 8 comparisons and 7 selects per byte, and compactc's own byte indexing adds 292 splits), 36 to get three bytes out of a 37-byte =authenticator_data=, 21 for the origin check. The fix is arithmetic, no new witnesses: recombine each 3-byte group big-endian and split it three times by six bits (62 splits for the whole encoding), and split only the 6-byte leftover limb of the authenticator data (5 splits). Lifted as =v3::base64url_32= — 40.8k rows less per P-256 circuit.
- *Every challenge-bearing circuit hashed a constant 64-byte domain tag in-circuit*: 3,750 rows. compactc does not constant-fold =persistentHash=. Lifted as =hash::Preimage= (the port's builder, with =uint= taking a typed =Uint<BITS>= so a hash limb cannot be wider than its slot) whose =hash= folds an all-immediate preimage off-circuit via =hash::constant_persistent_hash=, checked against the chip by simulation.
- *The JubJub small-order check was two scalar multiplications*: =ecMul(pk, 8) != ecMulGenerator(0)= is 402 + 688 + 402 + 688 + 15 ≈ 2.2k rows for a cofactor test. Three doublings and a coordinate compare are ~30. Lifted as =JubjubPoint::times_cofactor= / =is_identity= / =assert_not_small_order=; =times_cofactor= is checked equal to =ec_mul(p, 8)= by simulation on five points (the twisted-Edwards addition law is complete, so =add(p, p)= is doubling).
- *=kernel.caller= did not exist*, so the port hand-assembled thirteen Impact ops. Lifted as =kernel::caller= (=minocrab_ledger::kernel_caller=), byte-for-byte compactc 0.35.0's lowering, asserted by a test against the artifact's opcodes.
- Smaller: =B32::eq= (the port's =bytes_eq=, eleven uses), =Check::implies= (one select where =not(a).or(b)= is four instructions), =LedgerCell<Uint<N>>::add= (the port's hand-written =increment=), and the executor's FAB-atom normalisation (a real bug the port's fork fixed: an absent union arm must decode as the ledger's canonical empty atom) with its two regression tests.
- NOT lifted here: =GeneratorScalarTy for Secp256r1ScalarT= — the backend main is pinned to (04c9c5d) has no P-256 generator arm; that belongs with the zkir-3.1 bump.

* 3. The measured effect on the port

The port's scratch copy with the std helpers substituted (tags as immediates, =assert_not_small_order=, =base64url_32=, the limb-aware authenticator split, =when(..).otherwise(..)= in =deposit_unshielded=, =kernel::caller=), through the same cost model:

| | before | after |
|---+--------+-------|
| total rows, 52 circuits | 3,656,014 | 2,948,966 (−19.3%) |
| k lowered | — | 14 of 52 circuits |
| P-256 circuits at k=18 | 9 | 0 (all nine → 17) |
| JubJub k changes | — | append_inbox 16→15, remove_device 16→15, publish_recovery_session 16→15, withdraw_shielded_with_grant 17→16, withdraw_shielded_to_contract_with_grant 17→16, recover_finalise 12→11 |
| per P-256 circuit | | −34.4k to −40.8k rows |
| per JubJub device circuit | | −5.05k rows (tag fold 3.75k + cofactor check 1.3k) |
| per k256 device circuit | | −3.75k rows (the tag fold; the ECDSA floor is 23.4k) |

The k=17 → 16 that would matter most for k256 (=withdraw_shielded_with_k256=, 67.7k → 64.0k) did not happen: 2^16 is 65,536 and the backend's reserved rows put the real threshold near 61-62k, so another ~2.5k has to come from the protocol-fixed parts (nine SHA blocks and the ECDSA), which is to say from Compact-side spec changes (a 32-byte tag where 8 would do, preimages packed to block boundaries), not from the eDSL.

Soundness notes for the three big ones: the fold is SHA-256 over the same FAB binary encoding the chip hashes (=ValueReprAlignedValue::binary_repr=) and the test holds the two equal; base64url is exact integer arithmetic on byte values the circuit already range-checked (the 24-bit group is below the field, the splits are the backend's); the cofactor doubling relies on completeness of the JubJub addition law, true for twisted Edwards with non-square =d= and asserted by simulation — a real proof of =times_cofactor= against the backend's gadget is the remaining check before anyone relies on it for a stored key.
