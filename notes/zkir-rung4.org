#+title: M27 rung 4 — executable semantics: the intrinsics made concrete

Rung 3 (notes/zkir-semantics.org §4.1, §5) left ZKIR v3's intrinsics
UNINTERPRETED: =MinocrabZkir.Semantics= is =IrSource::preprocess=
transcribed arm by arm over the real =Instr=, with the hashes and the
curve operations as fields of a =Model= record over opaque =Carriers=.
Everything Native and Bytes32 was already concrete, so rung 3 was a real
interpreter for any circuit that used no hash and no curve — which is no
circuit anyone deploys.

Rung 4 fills the record in and proves the filled-in interpreter agrees
with the Rust reference on real preimages. This note is what was ported,
from where, what deliberately was NOT, what the corpus of records covers,
how to run the gate, and the four things that disagreed before they were
fixed.

Design of record for the interface itself is zkir-semantics.org §6; this
note records what was built against it and where the build departed from
the sketch.

* 1. The gate, in one block

#+begin_src sh
# the standing gate — plain `cargo test`, skips loudly without lake/corpus
cargo test -p minocrab-zkir --test lean_run
#   lean agrees with the rust oracle on 40 run records
#   (29 accepting, 11 rejecting) and 73 known-answer vectors

# the two halves, by hand
cd crates/minocrab-zkir/lean && lake build
./.lake/build/bin/zkir-run --kat differential/known-answers.txt
./.lake/build/bin/zkir-run \
  ../../../corpus/zkir/signet-midnight-examples/examples/erc20-vault/contract/src/erc20-vault/zkir/startSwap.zkir \
  differential/startSwap.record
#+end_src

CI's =lean= job runs =--kat= beside =zkir-roundtrip= / =zkir-wellformed=
and then =cargo test -p minocrab-zkir --test lean_run= for the per-record
loop, so CI and the local loop check the same thing.

Regenerating the records is two steps, both from the repo root:

#+begin_src sh
MINOCRAB_DUMP_PREIMAGES=$PWD/target/lean-differential/preimages \
  cargo test -p minocrab-contracts \
    --test erc20_vault_differential \
    --test signet_contract_differential \
    --test manager_differential
MINOCRAB_PREIMAGES=$PWD/target/lean-differential/preimages \
  cargo test -p minocrab-contracts --test lean_differential -- --ignored
#+end_src

* 2. The interface, as built

§6 said: a Rust regenerator, an =--ignored= test in the house snapshot
pattern, writing one PLAIN-TEXT run record per (side, circuit). Built as
=crates/minocrab-contracts/tests/lean_differential.rs=. Departures from
the sketch, each deliberate:

- ARTIFACTS ARE REFERENCED, NOT COPIED. §6 left open whether to commit a
  =.zkir= per record. Every circuit with a dumped preimage has a CORPUS
  twin — compactc's own artifact, already tracked under =corpus/zkir/= —
  and the differential suites already assert PI-equality of our artifact
  and that twin on exactly these preimages
  (=minocrab_sim::v3::assert_call_compatible=, which also checks
  =pi_skips= and cross-checks both sides against upstream
  =IrSource::check=). So a record names a repo-relative corpus path and
  the committed bytes are the record alone: 360K for forty of them, no
  duplicated artifacts. It also makes the gate a differential between
  COMPACTC'S artifact and our Lean, rather than between two things we
  wrote — strictly the better statement.
- CONSEQUENCE, stated so it is not discovered later: the gate needs the
  corpus, and skips loudly without it, exactly like =lean_roundtrip.rs=
  and =corpus_roundtrip.rs=.
- LEXEME: a field element is =0x= + little-endian hex bytes, minimal
  length, at least one byte — the artifact's own immediate spelling
  (=zkir-field-rep->string=, print-zkir-v3.ss:46-61; =immString= in
  Print.lean). A byte string is =#= + hex in stream order. Both sides
  have one implementation each and the KAT pins that they agree.
- WRAPPING: keys repeat, eight values a line; a bare key is an empty
  stream. A =pis= stream runs to hundreds of elements and a single line
  of 20K is not diffable.
- The expected block is everything from the =result= line on, and it is
  BYTE-EQUAL to =zkir-run='s entire stdout. No normalisation on either
  side, so nothing can be hidden in the comparison.
- A SECOND, lake-free test (=the_run_records_agree_with_the_rust_oracle=)
  re-derives every record's expected block from the artifact and preimage
  the record names. A hand-edited record, or a drift in =simulate=, fails
  plain =cargo test= without Lean in the picture at all.

* 3. The record corpus: what is in it and why

Forty records; 28 honest, 12 tampered; 29 accepting, 11 rejecting.

| side | records | why these |
|---|---|---|
| erc20-vault | 17 honest | every circuit of the M28 seventeen-circuit vault — the milestone's own target, and the only side where the leakage inventory's claims live |
| Signet singleton | 3 honest (=signBidirectional=, =respond=, =respondBidirectional=) | the emit-only shape: =add= / =mul= / =div_mod_power_of_two= / =impact= and nothing else, so a failure here is the WALK, not an intrinsic |
| AA-manager | 8 honest (=depositShielded=, and seven preimages of =execute=) | =depositShielded= is persistent_hash + transient_hash; =execute= is the only circuit in the workspace carrying keccak256 AND persistent_hash AND transient_hash AND a full secp256k1 ECDSA verification, and its seven preimages take seven different branches through it |

Intrinsic coverage, counted from the artifacts the records name:

| intrinsic | exercised by |
|---|---|
| =transient_hash= (Poseidon) | every vault circuit (56 sites across the seventeen), =manager_depositShielded=, =manager_execute_*= |
| =persistent_hash= (SHA-256 over the FAB bytes) | 36 sites in the vault; both manager sides |
| =keccak256= | =manager_execute_*= |
| secp256k1 =encode= / =decode= | =initialise= and the nine =complete*=/=refund*= vault circuits (a =Point<Secp256k1>= argument), =manager_execute_*= |
| secp256k1 =ec_mul=, =ec_mul_generator=, point =add=, =into_coordinates=, =into_bytes32=, =from_bytes32=, scalar =mul=/=inv= | the nine =complete*=/=refund*= vault circuits and =manager_execute_*= — the ECDSA block |
| the FAB alignment decode | every =persistent_hash= / =keccak256= site above, plus its own KAT vectors (§5) |
| Jubjub, secp256r1, curve25519 | NOTHING. See §4. |

The twelve tampered records perturb one element of one stream by +1:
=initialise= (transcript, inputs), =startWithdraw= (transcript, witness),
=completeWithdraw= (transcript, witness), =startSwap= (transcript),
=respond= (transcript), =signBidirectional= (inputs),
=manager_depositShielded= (transcript), =manager_execute_reg_native=
(transcript), =manager_execute_withdraw_shielded= (witness). An =inputs=
tamper additionally breaks the communications commitment, so the
epilogue's =transient_commit= equality is on the exercised path too.

ELEVEN of the twelve reject. The twelfth is a FACT, not a stale table:
=completeWithdraw.tamper-witness-0= is still ACCEPTED, because transcript
reads and their guards are unconstrained in the v3 statement
(zkir-semantics.org §4.3; upstream's own spec PR #16 §6.6(d) and O4 say
the same), and =completeWithdraw='s success branch takes no secret
witness — the unlinkability draft's Gap 2, §7 V5. The record carries the
accepting verdict, so the Lean has to reproduce the acceptance too; an
accepting tamper pins that fact as firmly as a rejecting one pins the
reject. The regenerator asserts only that at least eight tampers reject,
so the reject path cannot silently empty out.

* 4. Concrete vs uninterpreted, with citations

** Concrete (=MinocrabZkir.Intrinsics=)

| primitive | file | ported from |
|---|---|---|
| =transient_hash=, =transient_commit= | Poseidon.lean | transient-crypto/src/hash.rs:78-90 (rev 04c9c5d9) → midnight-circuits 7.2.4 =PoseidonChip=: mod.rs:135-141 (=HashCPU::hash=), poseidon_cpu.rs:127-183 (the sponge), :63-101 and :276-291 (the rounds and the SKIP-FREE permutation), constants/mod.rs:18-28 (WIDTH 3, RATE 2, 8 full, 60 partial), constants/blstrs.rs:28-1445 (204 round constants + 3x3 MDS) |
| =persistent_hash= | Sha256.lean | SHA-256 as such; ir_vm.rs:501 is =Sha256::digest= of the FAB bytes |
| =keccak256= | Keccak.lean | Keccak-256 in the ORIGINAL 0x01 padding (=sha3::Keccak256=, ir_vm.rs:502) — NOT FIPS 202's SHA3-256, which differs only in that byte |
| the FAB byte decode | Semantics.lean =fabAtom= / =fabBytes= | transient-crypto/src/fab.rs:337-345, 427-433, 513-527 and repr.rs:133-163 (off-circuit), ir_vm.rs:125-180 (in-circuit) |
| all secp256k1 | Secp256k1.lean | zkir-v3 =ir_instructions/{add,mul,neg,inv,eq,ec_mul,into_coordinates,from_coordinates,into_bytes32,from_bytes32,encode}.rs=; midnight-curves 0.3.1 k256/curve.rs:31-115; the emulated-field encoding from midnight-circuits field_chip.rs:130-165 + weierstrass_chip.rs:235-266 with params.rs:181-189/212-221 |

Two details of the Poseidon port worth having written down, because both
are load-bearing and neither is obvious:

- the permutation ported is =permutation_cpu_raw= (upstream's own test
  module), not the shipped =permutation_cpu=. They are equal — upstream's
  =cpu_test= asserts it — and the difference is the partial-round SKIP
  optimisation, an arithmetisation trick with no semantic content. The
  KAT vectors are computed through the SHIPPED path, so this is checked,
  not assumed.
- =HashCPU::hash= calls =init(Some(len))=, so the register starts at
  =[0, 0, len]= and the squeeze takes the fixed-length branch (no
  padding element). An EMPTY input therefore yields no chunk, hence no
  permutation, hence exactly =0=. A vector pins it.

And one of the secp256k1 port: a field element's public-input encoding
is of =element − 1= (=as_public_input= shifts for a unique-zero
representation), split base =2^64= into 4 limbs and packed 3 + 1, i.e.
=[low 192 bits, top 64 bits]= of the shifted value. The identity point
encodes as the shifted =(0, 0)= plus a flag byte of 1, and =decode=
tests that flag BEFORE the length, so a trailing 1 yields the identity
whatever precedes it. All three facts have vectors.

** Uninterpreted, and why

Jubjub (=hash_to_curve=, =jubjub_scalar_from_native=, the point/scalar
arithmetic and encodings), secp256r1, and curve25519 stay uninterpreted
at rung 4. §5's rule is explicit — port them ONLY if a record exercises
them — and none does: the vault, the Signet singleton and the AA-manager
between them contain no Jubjub, r1 or 25519 value. The one workspace
circuit that touches Jubjub (=opaque::op_jubjub=, an =encode= of a
Jubjub value) has no dumped preimage, because no differential suite
proves it against a compactc twin, so there is nothing to validate a
port against. Writing 300 lines of twisted-Edwards arithmetic that no
gate exercises is exactly the unvalidated code the rule exists to
prevent.

THE SILENCE IS ENFORCED. Two =Model= fields — =hashToCurve= and
=jubjubScalarFromNative= — return a carrier type, not an =Except=, so
they cannot signal failure; a program using them would run to a WRONG
answer rather than an error. =Concrete.unsupported= closes that before
the walk starts: a value of an uninterpreted type can only enter a
program through a declared input or output type, a =public_input= /
=private_input= / =from_bytes32= type annotation, or one of those two
instructions, and =unsupported= refuses all of them by name.
=zkir-run= exits 3 with the objection printed. The placeholder carriers
are =Unit= for the same reason: a slip cannot masquerade as a real
value for long.

Cost, since the brief asked for the reasoning: secp256k1 WAS ported
despite being the expensive one, because the vault's own =complete*= and
=refund*= circuits need it — nine of the seventeen — and =initialise=
needs its =decode= for a =Point<Secp256k1>= argument. Restricting the
record corpus to the eight k256-free vault circuits would have been the
cheap option and would have left the vault prong's own circuits outside
the executable semantics, which is the wrong trade at this rung.

* 5. Known answers: validating each primitive BEFORE the interpreter

73 vectors in =lean/differential/known-answers.txt=, printed by the Rust
reference and recomputed by =zkir-run --kat=. This exists so that a
whole-circuit disagreement is never the first thing that tells you a
primitive is wrong: a drifted primitive names itself, with its arguments,
before any circuit is walked.

| op | vectors | what they pin |
|---|---|---|
| =poseidon= | 9 | lengths 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 17 (either side of the rate, and several chunks), plus a 31-byte element |
| =poseidon_commit= | 1 | the opening LEADS the value (hash.rs:86-90) |
| =sha256=, =keccak256= | 12 each | lengths 0, 1, 3, 55, 56, 63, 64, 65, 135, 136, 137, 200 — both padding rules at and either side of both block sizes |
| =fab_bytes= | 8 | the corpus's own atom shapes (stray + chunks, chunks only, a lone stray, empty) AND the three rejecting shapes: a full limb at or above 2^248, a stray limb above its slot, too few limbs |
| =fab_field= | 4 | the 32-byte width, including a full-width element |
| =k256_gen_mul=, =k256_gen_mul_x= | 6 each | scalar multiplication and the =into_coordinates= + =into_bytes32= path the ECDSA block uses |
| =k256_add= | 4 | including a doubling (3+3), an identity operand (5+0), and identity+identity |
| =k256_scalar_from_bytes32=, =k256_base_from_bytes32= | 4 each | the reduction of a 32-byte value, including one above the group order |
| =k256_point_decode= | 3 | the identity flag, a real point, and an off-curve pair (rejects) |

Every one of them goes through the SAME functions =preprocess= calls —
=transient_hash=, =Sha256::digest=, =Keccak256::digest=,
=Alignment::parse_field_repr= + =binary_repr=, and zkir-v3's own
=*_offcircuit= helpers — so what is pinned is the reference's behaviour,
not a second opinion about it.

* 6. What disagreed, and why

Four things. Two were bugs in the Lean, one was a gap the reading closed,
one is a property of the circuit that the records now carry.

** D1 — secp256k1 scalar multiplication computed the BIT-REVERSED scalar

=Secp256k1.mulAux= indexed the scalar's bits by the recursion depth
(=bit k= at step =k + 1=) instead of by =255 − k=, so =s · P= came out as
=reverse_bits(s) · P=. Caught by =k256_gen_mul= on the FIRST run of the
KAT — the very first vector, =s = 1=, did not give the generator.

Worth recording because of how it reads: every structural property one
would check by eye holds. The identity is right at =s = 0=, the number of
doublings is right, termination is obvious, the loop shape is the
textbook one. Only a known answer distinguishes it. This is the case
for the KAT existing at all.

** D2 — the FAB limb decode TRUNCATED where both readings reject

=Semantics.fabAtom= built a =bytes n= atom's limbs with =natToLE=, which
silently truncates a field element carrying a byte above its slot. Both
of upstream's readings reject instead: off-circuit,
=bytes_from_field_repr= (transient-crypto repr.rs:133-163, reached from
=Alignment::parse_field_repr=) returns =None= when any byte at or above
index =k= is non-zero, which =preprocess= turns into "Inputs did not
match alignment" (ir_vm.rs:493-496); in-circuit,
=assigned_to_le_bytes(.., Some(k))= RANGE-CHECKS the value to =k= bytes
(ir_vm.rs:155-160). So the Lean accepted preimages the reference rejects.

Found by READING the off-circuit path, not by running the differential:
no corpus record hits it, because every honest limb is canonical and a
+1 tamper does not lift one over its slot. Fixed (=limbBytes=), and now
gated by the three rejecting =fab_bytes= vectors, which the pre-fix Lean
would fail.

The rung-3 note recorded =fabAtom= as transcribed from the IN-CIRCUIT
decoder while =run= models =preprocess=. The two agree on the byte
layout — stray bytes from the FIRST operand, full limbs in REVERSED
operand order, stray appended LAST — and now agree on rejection as well.

** D3 — CLOSED: zkir-semantics.org §10 I3, the =field= atom's width

§10 I3 flagged the =field= atom's 32-byte width as INFERRED from
=assigned_to_le_bytes(.., None)= rather than transcribed from the
off-circuit =parse_field_repr=. Reading it settles the question in the
Lean's favour: =AlignmentAtom::Field='s parse takes =res.as_le_bytes()=
and =normalize=s it (stripping trailing zeros), and =binary_repr= sends
it back through =value_atom_as_field= (=from_uniform_bytes= over a
64-byte buffer, which reduces to the same element for any canonical
=Fr=) and then =Fr::binary_repr=, which writes =as_le_bytes= —
=FR_BYTES= = 32 of them (transient-crypto repr.rs:49-55, curve.rs:194).
So a =field= atom is exactly the operand's 32 little-endian bytes. Four
=fab_field= vectors pin it. I3 can be struck.

** D4 — an accepting tamper, which is the circuit's property, not a defect

See §3: =completeWithdraw='s guarded-off success-branch witness can be
perturbed and the run still accepts. Not a disagreement between the Lean
and the Rust — they agree, and the record says =accept= — but it is the
first place in this repo where the unconstrained-transcript-read fact of
§4.3 shows up as an executable observation rather than a statement about
the constraint system. The unlinkability draft's Gap 2 (§7 V5) is the
same fact seen from the other side.

** Housekeeping found on the way

Three count assertions were stale on =main= and had to move in rung 4's
first commit: the AA-manager port and the signet corpus refresh took the
corpus to 814 files / 92 v3 / 16,666 instructions, while
=corpus_roundtrip.rs= still said (806, 84), =lean_roundtrip.rs= "84 ok",
and =lean_dataflow.rs= 16,815. The COMPARISONS were all green — 92 files
round-tripped byte-for-byte and the Lean and Rust dataflow dumps were
equal line for line — so nothing had regressed; only the numbers had.
Recorded because the house rule is that these move in the same commit as
the corpus, and this time they did not.

* 7. What rung 4 does NOT claim

- It is a DIFFERENTIAL, not a proof. What is established is that the Lean
  interpreter and the Rust reference (itself cross-checked against
  upstream =IrSource::check=) agree on 40 preimages of 22 distinct
  circuits, and that four ported primitives reproduce 73 reference
  answers. No theorem about =run= was proved here; rung 3's =Sat= and
  =completeness= are untouched.
- =SatCommitment= still needs =encode ∘ decode = id= on the raw inputs, a
  =Model= property. It is now CHECKABLE (the model is concrete) but it is
  not proved; the epilogue's commitment equality is exercised by every
  accepting record and by the =inputs= tampers, which is evidence, not a
  lemma.
- The eight uninterpreted foreign types are a real boundary, not a
  formality: any future circuit using Jubjub, secp256r1 or curve25519 is
  refused by =Concrete.unsupported= rather than approximated. The next
  time one appears with a dumped preimage, that is when it gets ported.
- Nothing here touches the pass theorems (=MinocrabProofs=), which are
  about IR-to-IR transformations and are unaffected by the model.
